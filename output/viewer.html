The idea

A two-storey carriage-house type. The print studio sits on the ground floor, where the concrete slab can carry an etching press (often 1,000 to 2,500 lb) and where an 8-foot sliding door lets you move the press, paper and flat files in and out. The ADU sits above with its own enclosed stair and street-side entry, so the dwelling never shares air with solvents or acid.

The studio's long north wall gets tall windows for even, shadow-free light over the inking table. Wet work (sink, fume hood, etching) is grouped in one room on the north side, directly under the ADU kitchen, so plumbing and exhaust stack.

Assumptions in this draft

  • Intaglio and relief printing with one etching press (30" × 60" bed), so the layout reserves a 12' × 7' clear zone for bed travel and walking around it.
  • Ferric chloride or similar etching in a ducted fume hood. No solvent-heavy screen washout booth yet.
  • One-bedroom ADU, about 750 sf, with a vaulted ceiling under the roof.
  • Flat site, with the street to the south. Wood frame on a slab-on-grade.
  • Footprint held at 36' × 24' to the outside of the sheathing, so the 9" walls take their thickness from the rooms.
  • Dimensions are in feet; the model origin is the building's southwest corner.

Exterior wall: 8" double-stud, dense-pack cellulose

Two rows of 2x4 studs at 24" on centre with a 1" gap between them make an 8" cavity filled with dense-pack cellulose. The gap means no stud runs from inside to outside, so the only thermal bridges are the plates, headers and rim. The outer row carries the floor and roof; the inner row is non-bearing and holds wiring and plumbing without breaching the air barrier. Overall thickness is 9" plus siding.

Questions for you

  1. Printmaking processes. Which do you do (etching, relief, lithography, screenprint, letterpress)? Each changes the press count, the wet room, and ventilation.
  2. Press. Do you own a press already? Its make, bed size and weight set the clear zone and the slab design.
  3. Site. Lot size, which way the street and north are, setbacks, and the local ADU rules on size and height. Many cities cap a two-storey ADU around 25 ft; this draft's ridge is 28 ft.
  4. Who lives upstairs? You, a tenant, or family? That decides whether the stair should also open into the studio (drawn now) or stay fully separate.
  5. Arrangement. Stacked like this, or side by side on one level (studio and ADU sharing a wall), which is easier for accessibility and heavy equipment?
  6. Teaching or open studio. Will other people use the studio? That brings accessibility, a larger WC and occupancy rules into play.
